# 00.5 — Target market and positioning

The founder's script for "who, why now, why us." Every decision in
this codebase — stack, pricing, feature scope, LLM choice — traces
back to answers here.

Companion doc: `Project_Docs/COMPETITORS.md`.

---

## Who we serve (Ideal Customer Profile)

**Firm size.** 50-1000 employees. Small enough that a Big-4 engagement
is unaffordable; big enough that a spreadsheet is dangerous.

**Firm type.**
- Payment institutions (PIs) under PSD2
- Electronic money institutions (EMIs)
- Small and medium-sized banks (< €30bn assets)
- Insurance intermediaries and MGAs
- Investment firms (Article 4(1)(1) MiFID II)
- Credit institutions outside the SSM significant-supervision list
- Fintechs and neobanks that have graduated past their sandbox licence

**Geography.** EU-27 + EEA (Norway, Iceland, Liechtenstein) + UK
insofar as UK firms passport into the EU. Data residency is a
regulatory line — everything stays in EU regions.

**Trigger event.** One of:
1. Just failed a national competent authority (NCA) review after the
   March 2026 submission.
2. Scraped through March 2026 with consulting hours and cannot afford
   to do that every quarter.
3. Growing across borders — the RoI complexity increases with every
   subsidiary.
4. New Head of Regulatory Reporting who inherited a spreadsheet and
   wants a real tool.

**Buyer.** Head of Regulatory Reporting or Chief Compliance Officer.
**Economic buyer.** COO or CFO. **Champion.** Regulatory analyst who
did the March 2026 reconciliation by hand. **Blocker.** CISO (if not
in the loop early on data handling).

**Buying budget.** €5,000-€15,000/year for a self-serve product;
€25,000-€60,000/year if we sell up-market with a small services
attach.

## Why now (market timing)

- **March 2026 was the first mandatory RoI submission** under DORA
  Article 28(3). It happened four months before this doc was written.
- **Only 6.5% of firms passed all 116 quality checks** in the
  ESMA/EIOPA/EBA dry-run. The industry knows it has a problem.
- **The next mandatory submission is Q1 2027.** Quarterly refreshes
  are already required in between.
- **NCA supervisory letters have started arriving** at firms with
  poor-quality submissions. Fines are not yet on the wire but the
  supervisory tone has shifted from "encouragement" to "expectation."
- **DORA is not going away.** Unlike a one-off migration project, this
  is an evergreen reporting obligation with rising quality bar.

That is a 24-month window (mid-2026 to mid-2028) where firms will
actively spend on tools rather than services.

## Why us (positioning)

The vendor landscape is:

- **Big and expensive** (OneTrust, ServiceNow, Diligent/3rdRisk after
  the Jan 2026 acquisition) — priced for tier-1 banks; 3-9 month
  sales cycle.
- **Broad and shallow** (Copla, Venvera) — DORA is one framework
  among sixteen; the RoI-specific workflow is thin.
- **DORA-native but old-school** (Vendorica) — template-driven,
  consulting-heavy, no AI extraction.
- **Cert-first, not DORA** (Vanta, Drata) — great UX, wrong regulation.
- **Consulting hours** (Deloitte, PwC, EY, KPMG) — expensive, does
  not scale to quarterly refreshes.

**EvidenceOS is the only product that is simultaneously:**
1. DORA-native and RoI-deep
2. Mid-market priced with self-serve onboarding
3. Evidence-first (every RoI cell traces to a source document)
4. Deterministic where regulators demand it (real EBA business-
   validation rules are code, not prompts)
5. Human-approved by design (nothing auto-finalises)

We do not out-feature Vanta; we out-regulate them. We do not out-spend
Deloitte on sales; we out-price them by an order of magnitude. We do
not out-brand Diligent; we out-focus them on the one workflow that
matters this year.

## Go-to-market plan (the realistic one)

**Phase A — walking-skeleton MVP.** ✅ **Done, ahead of schedule** (as of
2026-07-17, against an original "now to end of 2026" target). Not a
walking skeleton any more: full 14-real-EBA-table RoI coverage, 61
fields, deterministic validation against the actual EBA business rules
(not an approximation), human review workflow, draft xBRL-CSV export,
mobile-responsive redesign, live on staging *and* production. The
engineering-depth story (real regulatory source data, not guessed;
bugs found via real end-to-end verification, not just green tests) is
already strong enough to lead a design-partner conversation with, not
just an interview.

**Phase B — design partners.** Can reasonably start now rather than
waiting for Jan 2027, given Phase A finished early. Same target as
originally scoped: three firms, free tier, in exchange for feedback and
a case study — one payment institution in Ireland, one EMI in the
Netherlands, one insurance broker in Germany. See "Channels" below for
how to actually reach them.

**Phase C — first paid pilots.** Convert two design partners once
Phase B feedback is in. **Invoice/bank-transfer billing, not self-serve
Stripe** — at 2-3 customers, a self-serve checkout flow solves a
problem you don't have yet. See "Payments" below.

**Phase D — self-serve launch.** Public pricing page, self-serve
onboarding (this is when Stripe Billing/Checkout actually earns its
place), integrations with the top three contract management systems
(DocuSign CLM, Ironclad, LinkSquares).

The original MSc-portfolio framing ("stops at Phase A, enough to land a
senior engineering role or raise a pre-seed") still holds — Phase A
being done early just means that option is available sooner, not that
Phase B-D are mandatory next steps.

## Channels — how to actually reach the ICP

In order of effort-to-first-response for a solo founder:

1. **LinkedIn, direct.** Compliance officers post about DORA pain
   publicly — it's a live, shared frustration post-March-2026. Comment
   thoughtfully on their posts, then DM. Cheapest, fastest channel,
   matches how this ICP actually behaves online.
2. **Compliance/RegTech communities.** DORA-specific LinkedIn groups,
   national fintech associations (Ireland: FinTech Ireland;
   Netherlands: Holland FinTech) — low-cost, high-relevance.
3. **Warm intros via compliance consultancies.** The Deloitte/PwC/KPMG
   firms this doc positions against also have overflow clients they
   can't serve profitably at mid-market prices — a referral or
   goodwill relationship costs them nothing and gets us a warm lead.
4. **Content.** One sharp, specific post ("we parsed EBA's own
   validation-rules workbook and found X") is more credible to this
   audience than generic "AI compliance" marketing — the engineering
   deep-dives already written up in `README.md` are exactly this kind
   of material, lightly adapted.
5. **NCA-adjacent events/webinars.** DORA conferences in Ireland/
   Netherlands/Germany, matching the Phase B target geographies, are
   where the actual buyers are physically gathering right now.

Paid ads and SEO are the wrong tool for a 3-design-partner goal — too
slow and too broad. Revisit once Phase D (self-serve) is real.

## Payments — not yet, and that's the right call

**Don't wire up Stripe (or any payment processor) until Phase D.**
Phase C is 2-3 paid pilots — an invoice and a bank transfer handles
that with zero engineering effort and zero new compliance surface
(EU VAT handling, subscription billing logic, PCI scope even via a
processor like Stripe). Adding payment infrastructure before there's
a repeatable price and a channel producing inbound signups is solving
a problem that doesn't exist yet, at the cost of engineering time that
belongs on the product.

**When Phase D actually arrives**, the shape of the work: register a
business entity if one doesn't already exist (Stripe Atlas or a local
Irish company both fit — Ireland also matches the product's own
EU-data-residency story), verify it with Stripe directly (KYC, bank
account, tax ID — this is account creation the founder does
personally, not something to delegate), then Stripe Billing/Checkout
for subscriptions is a well-trodden integration, not a hard
engineering problem by the time it's actually needed.

## Interview / investor Q&A

**Q: Why not sell to enterprise directly? The ACVs are bigger.**
A: Enterprise has incumbents (OneTrust, ServiceNow, Diligent) with
existing procurement relationships. A solo/small founding team wins
mid-market with product-led growth, not enterprise with a sales army.
Move up-market after 100 mid-market customers.

**Q: What happens if a Big 4 firm launches a competing SaaS?**
A: They will. Deloitte OmniaAI already flirts with this space. Their
weakness is the same as Vanta's: distribution built for enterprise
clients means they cannot economically serve a €10k/year customer.
The mid-market is the moat.

**Q: How do you handle liability if your validation gets a customer
fined?**
A: (1) Deterministic checks reflect the RTS text; we can defend
every rule. (2) Human-in-the-loop means every finalisation is
customer-signed. (3) Standard SaaS liability caps in the MSA. (4)
E&O insurance covers residual risk.

**Q: What is the AI moat if any LLM can extract fields from a PDF?**
A: The moat is not extraction. It is: 42 real EBA business-validation
rules implemented deterministically (not "116" — that's the
regulator's full-submission bar across all 14 tables and three
different check layers, and we're precise about that distinction with
technical buyers, not loose with it), the audit chain that ties every
RoI cell to a source document with confidence and reviewer identity,
the RLS-secured workspace model, the xBRL-CSV export structure. AI is
a component, not the product.

**Q: What is your competitive advantage in 5 years when everyone has
this?**
A: Data. Once we have 500 firms' RoIs in one place, we know what a
"typical PI contract" looks like — that is a benchmark product, a
peer-comparison product, a supervisory-heatmap product. The product
that starts as compliance ends as market intelligence.

**Q: Will you always need a human in the loop?**
A: Yes, by regulation and by design. DORA governance requires named
accountability; auto-finalisation would strip that. But we can move
the human from "reviews every cell" to "reviews only cells with
confidence < 95%" — the ROI is in what the human does *not* have to
touch.
